Feeling Silly

Silly feelings can bring laughter, play, and friendship. Play is at its best when everyone feels safe, included, and ready to join in.

Try saying

"I have playful energy. Is this a good time for a silly game?"

Happy, respectful silliness

Joyful play

Funny voices, dance moves, and imagination can help us connect.

Read the room

Others may need calm, quiet, or focus, even when you feel playful.

Laugh with, not at

Kind silliness never embarrasses, hurts, or excludes someone.

Try this now

  1. Choose a playful minute. Invent a dance, a rhyme, or a funny drawing together.
  2. Check in. Ask, "Is this fun for you too?" and listen to the answer.
  3. Switch gears gently. When it is time to settle, take three breaths and choose a quiet activity.

For grown-ups

Playful energy is useful. Name when it fits, provide joyful outlets, and teach children how to notice other people's boundaries.
