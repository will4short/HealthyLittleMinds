Overview

Tiny steps and flexible plans that help kids and teens move toward what matters. This page offers simple, friendly ideas you can try at home or school. Every child is different—take what helps and leave the rest.

Try It Now

  • 1% Action: choose one 5-minute step toward a goal and do it now.
  • WOOP: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan—write one short line for each.
  • If-Then card: “If I get stuck, then I will ask for help or take a 2-minute reset.”

Talk Starters

  • “When you think about a goal, what does your body do to let you know—tense, excited, calm?”
  • “What tiny step could help a little, even if it doesn’t fix everything?”
  • “Who could we ask for support if we need more help?”

Build a Goal That Can Grow

Goals work best when they feel meaningful, small enough to begin, and flexible when plans change.

Meaning

Ask, “Why does this matter to me?” A goal connected to a value is easier to return to.

Small Steps

Turn “get better at reading” into “read two pages after dinner today.”

Setbacks

A hard day is information, not failure. Change the step, ask for support, and begin again.
